Coherence

← Back to Films

This is what it feels like when reality fractures.

A dinner party. A passing comet. The power goes out. Someone suggests checking the one house on the block that still has lights. Two people volunteer. They come back different.

Not wrong. Different.

Coherence doesn't explain. It traps you inside the panic. The confusion. The slow realization that the rules stopped applying and no one told you when.

They use glow sticks as markers. Blue. Red. Green. Color-coded realities. A desperate attempt to track which version of the night they're living. But the colors start bleeding together. The versions multiply. You lose track.

So do they.

The handheld camera never steadies. The house feels small. Claustrophobic. Everyone talks over each other. No one listens. The blocking is loose. The performances feel improvised. Raw. Like watching footage from an actual breakdown.

There are other versions of you out there. Walking through identical nights. Making slightly different choices. What happens when you meet them? What happens when one version decides another shouldn't exist?

The film doesn't comfort you. It doesn't resolve. It ends with a woman staring at her phone. Terrified of what she might see. Terrified she already knows.

Coherence is a thought experiment with a body count. It makes quantum mechanics feel visceral. Real. Dangerous.

You'll spend the next week wondering which version of yourself made it to the end of the day.

Check the glow stick. Make sure it's the right color.
